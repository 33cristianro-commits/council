# What's Capturing Attention on Shorts — Sept 12 to Oct 12, 2026

Research brief for a faceless, AI-produced Shorts channel. Compiled 2026-10-12.

## How this was gathered (and its limits)

- YouTube and TikTok could not be opened directly from the research environment, so this is built from
  web search across trend trackers, news coverage (Fast Company, AFP), Know Your Meme and creator blogs.
- Trend blogs often copy each other. View counts below are as reported by the cited source, not verified.
- Before copying any trend, open its sound or hashtag page on TikTok/YouTube and check it is still climbing.
  Most audio trends have a 1-3 week life.

### 1. AI character series — the biggest AI-native hit of 2026
- **Fruit Love Island** (account "Ai Cinema"): AI fruit characters in a *Love Island* parody. Launched
  March 13, 2026; passed 3M followers in about a week; new episodes routinely pulled tens of millions of
  views; top episode ~38.7-39M views. Over 300M total views reported.
- **Layout:** recurring cast with distinct personalities → drama/conflict each episode → ends on an
  unresolved question → "Episode N" in the title so viewers binge.
- **The warning:** 12 of 22 episodes were taken down on TikTok and the YouTube account was removed. It was
  a parody of a real, trademarked show. **Your series must be an original concept**, not a copy of an
  existing show.
- **Micro-drama structure** (the format behind it): open on a powerful event, deliver new information
  fast, end on a new unanswered question. YouTube now offers **Shorts Series** to group Shorts into
  seasons with sequential playback, which is built exactly for this.

### 2. AI persona — Jean Phil ("Jean Philanthrope")
- First posted Sept 17, 2026 (Instagram, TikTok two days later). One clip reportedly hit ~35.9M views;
  the account passed 228K followers within days.
- **Why it worked:** a single, instantly recognisable look (symmetrical blond bob, curled moustache,
  formal suits) and the comment-section war over "is he real or AI?" Arguments in comments push videos
  to more feeds.
- **Copycats appeared within days** (Archibald Brown, Derek Mercer). Being first to a format matters.
- **The warning:** the account promoted a meme coin that lost more than 99% of its value the same day.
  Build a character, never a token or a "get rich" pitch.

### 3. Ramalama costume walk / "Did I win this trend?"
- Audio: Róisín Murphy, "Ramalama (Bang Bang)". One tracker counts ~430K videos using it this October.
- **Shot by shot:** camera low (legs/shoes only) → slow, heavy walk toward the lens on the beat (dragging a
  prop optional) → on the drop, tilt up or zoom out to the full costume → no on-screen text, the reveal
  carries it. Spooky location (garage, stairwell, hallway). Caption "Did I win this trend?" invites votes.
- **AI angle:** do it with *your* AI character from #1 or #2. Their costume reveal doubles as character
  promotion. Ends Oct 31 (Halloween is a Saturday this year).

### 4-6. Audio and text formats (fast, cheap, short-lived)
- **Deadpan setup → bass drop:** state something calmly, the drop reveals the twist. "My American Girl
  Doll" uses a 2009 childhood clip to reveal grown-up versions of prized possessions.
- **"-A" text hook:** a text-message conversation on screen, signed off "-A" over the Pretty Little Liars
  audio. Pure text, so the AI can make it end to end.
- **"Two Fishes":** text on screen sets up a take ("me explaining I can save money and still travel"),
  then a deadpan lip-sync. Attribution of the line is disputed between sources (Corbin Mims vs. Sincere).

### Upcoming moments to plan for
- **Halloween**, Sat Oct 31: spooky content peaks the week before.
- **World Series**, starts Oct 23.

## 2. Anatomy of a high-retention Short (layout to copy)

Numbers come from creator-tool blogs and are rules of thumb, not YouTube-official.

| Time | What happens |
|------|--------------|
| 0-2s | **Hook.** Open on the most dramatic frame or line. No intro, no logo. YouTube's hook window is ~2s. |
| 2-6s | **Promise.** Make clear what the viewer gets by staying ("wait for her answer…"). |
| Middle | **Payoff build.** A visual change every 1-2s; captions always on screen. |
| Last 2s | **Payoff + loop.** Final line flows into the first, so the video replays. Series: end on a question. |

- **Length:** sources disagree, but the clustered sweet spot is **25-45s** for one-off Shorts and
  **60-90s** for story episodes. Sub-15s clips reportedly lost reach in 2026, except quick audio-trend
  memes.
- **Retention target (rule of thumb):** about 65%+ average viewed for under-30s Shorts, about 50%+ for
  30-60s. Check your own YouTube Studio numbers, not blog figures.
- **Disclosure:** tick YouTube's altered/synthetic-content box for realistic AI. Faceless AI channels get
  throttled for skipping it and for repetitive output, not for being faceless.

## 3. Recommended play

**Build one original AI character (or a small cast), then point every trend at them.**

1. **Core:** an original AI series with numbered episodes, 60-90s, a cliffhanger every episode, and
   grouped with Shorts Series. This is the format with the proven AI-native ceiling (Fruit Love Island)
   and the best defence against the "inauthentic content" rule, because each episode is a new original
   story.
2. **Traffic:** put the same character through each week's trending audio and formats (Ramalama reveal
   now, the next sound next week) in 10-20s clips. These are cheap, and they funnel viewers to the series.
3. **Volume:** 3-5 posts a day to start (1-2 episodes and 2-3 trend clips), then scale whatever is
   retaining best.

**Avoid:** parodying real trademarked shows; meme coins or "money" pitches; reposting other creators'
clips; Reddit read-alouds; AI slideshows; one template copied 10 times a day.
